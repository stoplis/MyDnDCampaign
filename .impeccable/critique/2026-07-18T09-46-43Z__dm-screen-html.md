## Anti-Patterns Verdict

### LLM assessment

This does not read as generic AI-generated UI. The Gilded Ledger direction is specific, the three-pane composition follows a real DM workflow, and the dedicated combat workspace is more purposeful than a generic dashboard. The risk is product accretion: multiple generations of combat selectors, late token overrides, tiny operational labels, ornamental side stripes, custom scrollbars, and inconsistent interactive semantics make a bespoke tool feel patched rather than governed by a stable system.

## Overall Impression

The DM Screen is already the right conceptual north star: calm, literary, table-specific, and organized around the DM's real context. Its biggest opportunity is to turn that strong composition into a governed operational system—safe under pressure, keyboard-legible, responsive by structure, and clean enough internally that every other screen can inherit it without inheriting its drift.

## What's Working

1. **The information architecture mirrors the job.** Party health, narrative context, encounters, and bestiary remain adjacent, reducing context switching during play.
2. **The visual direction has a point of view.** Paper, ink, hairlines, restrained gold, and one serif voice avoid generic SaaS and bright fantasy-game styling.
3. **Combat earns a dedicated mode.** Moving combat into a full-screen workspace is the correct response to its higher information and action density.

## Cognitive Load

The interface fails five of eight checklist items: single focus, chunking, one thing at a time, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure. Grouping and context retention are comparatively strong, and the base visual hierarchy is credible.

The highest-load decision points exceed four visible options:

- The combat detail combines AC, HP, maximum HP, temporary HP, damage, healing, full heal, conditions, advantage/disadvantage, faction, removal, and a full reference block.
- The combat header combines exit, round controls, encounter identity, initiative rolling, and combatant addition.
- The chapter drawer exposes a full grid of chapters at once.
- The permanent shell combines four party records, chapter content, two right-rail modes, reset, chapter switching, and Start Combat.

This density is defensible for a practiced power tool only when hierarchy, keyboard paths, and safety feedback are exceptional. They are not there yet.

## Emotional Journey

- **Preparation:** The ledger aesthetic feels personal and prepared.
- **Exploration:** The three-pane shell provides reassuring situational awareness.
- **Entering combat:** The full-screen transition appropriately signals a new mode, but control density increases hesitation.
- **Making a mistake:** Confidence drops sharply because reset, removal, and combat closure appear immediate and difficult to recover.
- **Using a smaller screen:** The command-center model becomes a long stack, making the DM hunt for context instead of staying ahead of the table.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] High-cost actions lack a safety and recovery model

**Why it matters:** `Reset saved state` is permanently visible in the top bar, while removing a combatant and closing combat are immediate. A stray click during live play can destroy context or state at the moment Stephen is most cognitively loaded.

**Fix:** Move reset into a lower-emphasis settings/overflow area; distinguish "leave tracker" from "end combat"; confirm true resets; use a short-lived undo toast for combatant removal; preserve a recoverable previous combat snapshot.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden dm screen`

### [P1] Core interactions lack dependable keyboard and assistive semantics

**Why it matters:** Party, bestiary, and initiative rows rely on click handlers on non-interactive elements; popovers are generated div overlays without dialog semantics or focus management; visible focus treatment is sparse; drag reordering has no keyboard equivalent. This blocks accessibility and slows expert operation.

**Fix:** Use native buttons/links for rows or implement complete keyboard semantics; add accessible names; implement dialog roles, initial focus, Escape close, focus trap, and focus restoration; add keyboard initiative movement with announced positions; standardize `:focus-visible` across every control.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit dm screen`

### [P1] Responsive behavior abandons the command-center model

**Why it matters:** At 980px the shell becomes party rail, notes, and right rail in a vertical stack; combat becomes list above detail. The content survives, but the spatial model that makes the DM Screen useful disappears.

**Fix:** Retain one primary pane and move party/reference rails into explicit drawers or sheets at tablet widths. In combat, use an Initiative/Combatant segmented view or keep active detail sticky while round controls remain persistent. Verify touch targets and ensure the fixed combat action never covers content.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt dm screen`

### [P2] Operational hierarchy is too flat in the densest moments

**Why it matters:** Numerous 9–11px labels, one typographic voice without enough role differentiation, and many adjacent combat actions make scanning slower under variable table lighting. Source Serif 4 is the correct brand decision; its scale, weight, case, and numeric treatment need more discipline.

**Fix:** Keep the One Voice Rule, but raise the minimum secondary label size, strengthen the separation between prose and live numeric controls, align changing values consistently, make HP/turn actions dominant, and progressively disclose faction/reference editing.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout dm screen`

### [P2] The north-star stylesheet is not yet a stable source of truth

**Why it matters:** `css/dm-console.css` contains repeated generations of combat layout rules, duplicate selectors, a terracotta accent near the top overridden by gold late in the file, dormant themes while JavaScript forces light mode, `transition: all`, and decorative side-stripe variants. Propagating this directly would reproduce drift across every tool.

**Fix:** Consolidate tokens at the root, remove dead/duplicate theme and combat layers, use semantic z-index and motion tokens, transition named properties only, replace decorative side stripes with full-border/background/state treatments, and establish reusable shell/control primitives before other screens adopt the system.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish dm screen`

## Persona Red Flags

### Alex / Stephen — Impatient power user running a live session

- No keyboard path is evident for the primary workflow or initiative reordering.
- The top-level reset action and immediate combatant removal are dangerous under time pressure.
- Dense same-family labels and controls slow scanning of HP, AC, initiative, and conditions.
- Absolutely positioned condition/add menus risk clipping inside constrained layouts.
- Persistence is silent enough that Stephen cannot quickly distinguish saved, recoverable, and discarded state.

### Sam — Keyboard, screen-reader, or low-vision user

- Click-only div rows are not reliably focusable or announced as controls.
- Generated popovers do not expose a dependable dialog lifecycle.
- Selected state can rely on a narrow accent stripe or color.
- Many 9–11px labels and muted ink roles are fragile in variable lighting and at zoom.
- Forced light mode can glare at a dim table despite dormant dark/parchment tokens.

## Minor Observations

- “Back” should name its destination.
- The chapter drawer should prioritize current/recent chapters before the complete grid.
- Placeholder and tertiary text contrast should be verified, not inferred from token intent.
- Custom WebKit scrollbars are unnecessary and may reduce discoverability.
- `transition: all` should become named state transitions with reduced-motion handling.
- Context menus should escape clipping contexts through a top-level popover/portal layer.
- Hidden story content must remain excluded from keyboard and assistive exposure until expanded.
- Gold should indicate interaction/selection only; faction and danger colors must remain semantically separate.

## Questions to Consider

- If Stephen misclicks once at the table, which action can ruin the evening?
- Is the combat screen primarily a reference document or a turn-by-turn control surface?
- If the three-column spatial model is the product's north star, how should it translate rather than disappear on a tablet?
- Would a new user know what is clickable without hovering every row?
- Which primitives should every other screen inherit: tokens only, or also the shell, controls, overlays, and responsive rules?
